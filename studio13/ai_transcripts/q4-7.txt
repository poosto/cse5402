⏺ Yes. Q3 uses much less Arc/Mutex than your studio 12 code, and the Q4 code I
  just wrote falls between the two. The main reason is how closely each one
  follows the studio 12 spec.

  Studio 12 (your code, final version)
  - Every waiter is an Arc<Mutex<Waiter>>, and the chef is an Arc<Mutex<Chef>>.
  - Restaurant holds the waiters three ways: a Vec of them, a HashMap from table
    numbers to them, and a Mutex<VecDeque<...>> leader queue with a Condvar.
  - Customers use a RefCell for their order items.

  The spec pushes you toward all of this. It asks for:
  - a waiter struct that keeps its own thread handle, with activate and finish
    methods
  - a restaurant that holds "references" to the waiters
  - a table-to-waiter map
  - waiters calling pickup on each other

  Several threads have to reach the same waiter object, so each waiter needs
  Arc, and its fields need a Mutex.

  Q3 unattended (restaurant/)
  - Uses one Arc<Pass>, and the Pass holds one Mutex plus two Condvars for
    Leader/Followers. That's all the shared state.
  - A waiter is moved into its thread by value (waiter.work(kitchen, pass) takes
    self). Nothing else ever touches it, so it needs no lock.
  - main holds the JoinHandles. Waiters have no activate/finish.
  - Waiters reach the kitchen through a cloned Sender called KitchenProxy. The
    "Active Object" is the kitchen, not the waiters.
  - It also leaves out parts of the spec:
    - no Customer or OrderItem per seat (one order per table with a customer
      count)
    - no Food enum
    - no restaurant table-to-waiter map
    - no waiter-to-waiter pickup

  So it skips the shared-object structure that forced studio 12 to use
  Arc<Mutex<>>. It shares memory by communicating, as you said in your write-up.
  But that's partly because it solved a simpler problem than the spec asks for.
